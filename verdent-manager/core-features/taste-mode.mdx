Taste Mode is Verdent's design-first creation path for new apps with a visible interface. Use it when you have an idea in words, but not a finished design, screenshot, style guide, or complete page plan yet.

Instead of jumping straight into code, Taste Mode helps you decide what the product should look and feel like first, then turns the approved direction into a working app.

## When to use Taste Mode

Use Taste Mode when you want to create a new product UI from a text brief, such as:

- a full-stack app
- a web frontend or landing page
- a mobile app
- a multi-screen game or interactive experience
- a 0-to-1 product idea where the visual direction is not decided yet

## When to skip it

Skip Taste Mode when:

- the project is a trivial single-screen utility, such as a basic form or very small toy app
- you already have a complete design brief with a concrete style direction, palette, type system, and page or screen list
- you already have design files, mockups, screenshots, or a style guide that should be followed directly
- you explicitly want to skip design and go straight to code

<Note>
If your brief is already specific enough, Verdent can use it as the chosen direction and move directly toward page planning and build work.
</Note>

## How Taste Mode works

<Steps>
  <Step title="Describe the product">
    Start with a plain-language brief: what you want to build, who it is for, what it should do, and any mood or style references you already have.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Choose a visual direction">
    Verdent explores distinct design directions so you can pick the look and feel before code is written.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Plan the pages or screens">
    For multi-page, full-stack, or more complex products, Verdent turns the chosen direction into a page or screen plan and a plain-language product spec.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Build from the approved direction">
    After the direction is clear, Verdent restores the design into a working app and keeps later decisions tied to the approved design and spec.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Preview and continue">
    Review the app in preview, then continue with backend work, testing, or further iteration as needed.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## How to use Taste Mode in Verdent Desktop

When you start a new product in Manager, describe the app you want to build instead of choosing a template. If you do not already have a concrete design direction, Verdent can use Taste Mode to help you pick one before it builds.

You can keep the brief simple, but make it specific enough to guide taste decisions:

- what the product is
- who will use it
- the main job it should help them do
- the pages, screens, or states you already know you need
- any tone or style references, such as editorial, playful, functional, premium, minimalist, or bold
- anything you do not want, such as generic gradients, stock-looking imagery, or a certain visual style

## Tips for better results

- Say who the product is for.
- Describe the feeling you want, not just the feature list.
- Mention known pages or screens if you have them.
- Share references or constraints early if they matter.
- Say what to avoid if you already know what would feel wrong.
- For complex products, expect to confirm the page list and product spec before the full build continues.
